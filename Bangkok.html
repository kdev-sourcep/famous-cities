      Bangkok

      Thailand's electric capital mixes glittering temples, floating markets and rooftop bars with some of the best street food on the planet. It is ranked #8 on Time Out's Best Cities 2026 list.

      Overview

      Why visit Bangkok

      Bangkok became Thailand's capital in 1782, when King Rama I moved the royal court across the Chao Phraya River. The dazzling Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew, home of the revered Emerald Buddha, date from that time, while nearby Wat Pho houses a giant reclining Buddha and Wat Arun rises from the opposite riverbank.

      Locals call the city Krung Thep, the "City of Angels". Getting around is part of the adventure: hop on the BTS Skytrain or MRT to beat the traffic, ride the express boats along the river, or take a tuk-tuk for a short, bumpy thrill.

      Food is the city's true attraction. Graze on pad thai, som tam and mango sticky rice at street stalls, explore the night-time food scene of Yaowarat (Chinatown), and shop at the enormous Chatuchak Weekend Market. For a day out, visit the ancient temples of Ayutthaya or a floating market outside the city.

      Fun facts

      Things you didn't know about Bangkok

      1. 01

        The World'S Longest City Name

        Bangkok's full ceremonial name is listed by Guinness World Records as the longest place name in the world.

      2. 02

        Locals Call It Krung Thep

        Thais usually call their capital Krung Thep, meaning "City of Angels". "Bangkok" is the name mostly used by foreigners.

      3. 03

        A 46-Metre Reclining Buddha

        Wat Pho is home to a gold-leaf reclining Buddha 46 metres long. The temple is also known as a historic centre of traditional Thai massage.

      4. 04

        The Emerald Buddha Isn'T Emerald

        The Emerald Buddha at Wat Phra Kaew is carved from green stone. Its golden costume is changed by the King with the seasons, three times a year.

      5. 05

        One Of The World'S Most Visited Cities

        Bangkok regularly ranks among the most visited cities on the planet in international tourism rankings.

      6. 06

        A Giant Weekend Market

        Chatuchak Weekend Market has more than 15,000 stalls selling everything from clothes and plants to antiques and street food.

      7. 07

        The "Venice Of The East"

        Bangkok was once crisscrossed by canals called khlongs, and some neighborhoods still rely on boats today.

      8. 08

        A Temple Covered In Porcelain

        Wat Arun, the Temple of Dawn, is decorated with thousands of pieces of colorful Chinese porcelain that sparkle in the sun.

      9. 09

        Capital Since 1782

        King Rama I founded the current capital on the east bank of the Chao Phraya River in 1782, the start of the Rattanakosin era.

      10. 10

        Street Food With A Michelin Star

        Street-food legend Jay Fai earned a Michelin star in 2018 for her famous crab omelettes.
