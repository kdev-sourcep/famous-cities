PHNOM PENH











POPULATION

METRO: 2,300,000

The riverside capital of golden spires and heavy history — royal palaces, Khmer Rouge memorials, and the gateway to Angkor.

HIGHLIGHT RANKING

Methodology

Phnom Penh sits where the Mekong, Bassac and Tonlé Sap rivers meet — the 'four faces' the city is named for. The Royal Palace's golden spires and the Silver Pagoda rise over a riverside promenade that fills with dancers at sunset.

The city asks visitors to remember: the Tuol Sleng museum (a school turned Khmer Rouge prison) and the Choeung Ek killing fields confront the 1975–79 genocide that emptied the city. It's sobering, essential context — and it makes the country's warmth hit harder.

Speed's 2024 tour streamed from Phnom Penh. The city is also the practical gateway — most travellers continue to Siem Reap for Angkor Wat's temple mountains.

FUN FACTS

FOUR FACES

The city sits at the chaktomuk — the 'four faces' junction of three rivers.

SILVER PAGODA

The palace temple's floor is laid with over 5,000 silver tiles.

THE KILLING FIELDS

Choeung Ek and Tuol Sleng memorialise the Khmer Rouge genocide.

A CITY EMPTIED

The Khmer Rouge evacuated all of Phnom Penh in 1975 — it was a ghost town for years.

REVERSED RIVER

The Tonlé Sap river reverses direction each monsoon — a unique hydrology.

WAT PHNOM

The hilltop temple named for founding grandmother Penh is the city's origin legend.

FRENCH COLONIAL

Art Deco Central Market's yellow dome dates to 1937.

THE ANGKOR GATEWAY

Siem Reap and Angkor Wat are a short flight or bus away.

TUK-TUK CAPITAL

Remork tuk-tuks are the transport of choice — cheap and everywhere.

RIVERSIDE NIGHTS

The Sisowath Quay promenade fills with families and street food at dusk.
