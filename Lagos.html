LAGOS











POPULATION

METRO: 16,000,000

Africa's biggest city — an island-and-lagoon megacity running on hustle, Afrobeats, jollof rice and traffic locals call 'go-slow'.

HIGHLIGHT RANKING

Methodology

Lagos is Africa's largest city and its loudest — an estimated 16-plus million people spread across islands, lagoons and mainland suburbs linked by causeways and the famous 'go-slow' traffic jams.

The city runs on hustle and sound: Afrobeats was born here, Nollywood turns out thousands of films a year, and the markets — Balogun's fabric maze, the Lekki craft market — sell everything. At night the mainland's live-music bars and Victoria Island's clubs compete for the crowd.

Speed's 2025 Africa stop streamed from Lagos to ecstatic crowds — one of the biggest turnouts of the trip. Visitors get Kalakuta (Fela Kuti's house-museum), Africa's longest canopy walkway at Lekki Conservation Centre, and boat escapes to Tarkwa Bay.

FUN FACTS

AFRICA'S BIGGEST CITY

Estimates pass 16 million — the continent's largest urban area, still growing fast.

AFROBEATS' BIRTHPLACE

Fela Kuti's Lagos Shrine invented Afrobeat; today's Afrobeats stars took it global.

THE CITY OF ISLANDS

Victoria Island, Ikoyi and Lagos Island connect by bridges over the lagoon.

NOLLYWOOD'S ENGINE

Nigeria's film industry — one of the world's largest by output — is headquartered here.

THE GO-SLOW

Traffic jams are so famous they have a local name — pack snacks for the commute.

FELA'S HOUSE

The Kalakuta Republic Museum preserves the musician's home and stage.

A CANOPY WALK

Lekki Conservation Centre's 401-metre boardwalk is Africa's longest canopy walkway.

THE JOLLOF WAR

Lagos claims the best jollof rice — a proud feud with Accra.

THE FLOATING SCHOOL

Makoko's stilt village on the lagoon built a famous floating school.

DANFO YELLOW

The battered yellow danfo buses are the city's veins — packed, loud, ever-present.
