      Overview

      Why visit Barcelona

      Barcelona is an almost ideal European city — near-perfect weather, three miles of beaches within city limits, iconic parks and Gaudí’s surreal architecture in neighborhoods that march to their own beat. Which is exactly why it has also become ground zero for Europe’s overtourism debate, with roughly 12 million visitors a year for a city of 1.6 million.

      The response is now policy, not just protest. Barcelona has raised its tourist tax to among Europe’s highest, capped cruise passengers, banned new hotels in the center — and most dramatically, announced it will phase out all tourist-apartment licenses by late 2028. Practical takeaway for 2026: book legitimate accommodation early and expect it to cost more.

      The Gaudí skyline is nearing completion: the Sagrada Família’s central Tower of Jesus Christ — set to make it the tallest church in the world — is targeted to finish around the centenary of Gaudí’s death, with the full basilica following in the 2030s.

      Meanwhile the superblock program keeps converting streets into pedestrian plazas, bike lanes crisscross the city, and green corridors like Passeig de Sant Joan show what a car-light future looks like. Visit outside the July–August crush, buy skip-the-line tickets for the Sagrada Família and Park Güell well ahead, and save time for the neighborhoods — Gràcia, Poble-sec and Sant Antoni — where locals actually live.

      Fun facts

      Things you didn't know about Barcelona

      1. 01

        Sagrada Família Has Been Under Construction Since 1882

        Antoni Gaudí took over the basilica in 1883 and worked on it for more than 40 years until his death in 1926. Construction has continued ever since, funded largely by visitors' tickets.

      2. 02

        Gaudí'S Works Are A UNESCO World Heritage Site

        Park Güell, Casa Batlló, Casa Milà (La Pedrera), Palau Güell, Casa Vicens and the Nativity façade and crypt of the Sagrada Família are listed together by UNESCO as the "Works of Antoni Gaudí".

      3. 03

        The City Was Founded By The Romans

        Barcelona began as the Roman colony of Barcino more than 2,000 years ago. You can still walk through excavated Roman streets beneath the Barcelona History Museum in Plaça del Rei.

      4. 04

        Catalan Is The City'S Own Language

        Barcelona is the capital of Catalonia, and Catalan is co-official alongside Spanish. Most street signs and menus appear in Catalan first.

      5. 05

        The Beaches Were Transformed For The 1992 Olympics

        Much of Barcelona's waterfront was industrial until it was redeveloped for the 1992 Summer Olympics, opening the city up to the sea.

      6. 06

        The Eixample Is A Planned Grid Of Octagons

        Engineer Ildefons Cerdà designed the Eixample district in 1859. Its blocks have cut-off corners, turning every intersection into a small open square.

      7. 07

        Park Güell Was Meant To Be A Housing Estate

        Eusebi Güell planned it as an exclusive residential development, but only two houses were ever built. It later became a public park.

      8. 08

        La Boqueria Is Centuries Old

        The famous market off La Rambla traces its history back to the Middle Ages, when farmers sold produce outside the old city walls.

      9. 09

        Camp Nou Is Europe'S Largest Stadium

        FC Barcelona's home ground holds close to 100,000 fans, making it the biggest football stadium in Europe.

      10. 10

        The Magic Fountain Was Built For A World'S Fair

        The Magic Fountain of Montjuïc was designed by Carles Buïgas for the 1929 International Exposition and still puts on music-and-light shows today.
