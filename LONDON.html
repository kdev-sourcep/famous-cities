      Overview

      Why visit London

      London’s momentum has only grown. The Elizabeth line transformed cross-city travel — Heathrow to Tottenham Court Road in about 35 minutes — and visitor numbers have roared back to over 20 million international arrivals a year, keeping London the most-visited city in Europe.

      The skyline story continues on the south bank of the Thames, where the restored Battersea Power Station has reopened as a shopping, dining and entertainment district complete with a chimney-top viewing lift. At the Olympic Park, the new East Bank cultural quarter keeps opening — V&A East and Sadler’s Wells East are already drawing crowds.

      Theatre, museums and food remain world-beating: the West End sells out nightly, the British Museum and Tate Modern are still free, and the dining scene spans century-old chophouses to food halls across Borough Market, Spitalfields and Seven Dials.

      For first-timers: book the Tower of London and West End shows ahead, tap in with contactless payment straight on the Tube (no Oyster card needed), and leave time for the neighborhoods — Greenwich, Notting Hill, Shoreditch and Hampstead Heath each feel like their own village.

      Fun facts

      Things you didn't know about London

      1. 01

        London The Smallest City In England!

        That's right, The megacity the most of us know as London is actually called 'Greater London', whilst the City of London itself is a small portion of this sprawling metropolis. It has a population of only 9,123 people, making it the smallest in England

      2. 02

        Technically A Forest City

        We've spoken before about how green the capital is. It's hard to grasp unless you've visited , but the amount of greenery you can enjoy in London is enormous. So much so, that the density of the trees it has per square mile qualifies it as a forest as per the United Nations definition

      3. 03

        Half Of London Underground Runs Over Ground

        So really it should be called the Overground. Except we already have an Overground. Ah, well I think I'll stick to calling it 'the tube'.

      4. 04

        'Big Ben' Ain'T Really Big Ben

        Tourists from all over come to stand on Westminster Bridge and take a selfie next to Big Ben and the Houses of Parliament. Unfortunately, not one of them ever leaves with a picture of it. This world-famous landmark actually named the Elizabeth Tower - Big Ben is the name of the bell that lives inside. So you won't snap a photo of Big Ben but if you're lucky you might get to hear it

      5. 05

        London Ain'T That Rainy

        Rumor says that London is quite rainy but the opposite is actually true. The capital may be known for being a rainy city except for the fact, that its NOT TRUE AT ALL! London actually gets less rain than Rome, Miami, and Sydney both in terms of volume and number of rainy days.

      6. 06

        More Indian Restaurants Than In Mumbai

        Even though technically every restaurant in Mumbai is an Indian restaurant, London seems to love them more. They have many excellent eateries that it far outweighs its culinary rival. If you want a great curry, you're off going to London over India.

      7. 07

        Dont Expect Traffic To Get Better Any Time Soon

        If, that is, you're going by speed of movement across the city. vehicles driving through the city center average a top speed of 7.4 miles per hour - about the same speed as a horse and cab. Pet peeve we suggest you walk the glorious city

      8. 08

        London The Right Way

        Everybody knows that the English drive on the left-hand side of the road. Though, the same can't be said at "Savoy Court." This is where visitors can get back to their roots. Savoy Court is the a road off the Strand on which the drivers are required  by law to drive on the right-hand side of the road

      9. 09

        There Ain'T One London, But We Sure Are The Original

        This shouldn't come as surprise to the Americans who have a ton of cities and town names named  after famous cities around the world just like Paris, TX or Warsaw, KY. When it comes to London it is no different except it seems like France and Canada want to join in to share the glorious name of London as well.  Obviously the U.S. has 10 called London ,  Canada has 1 , and France has 1. That is a total of 13 Londons ! There is even an asteroid called London. Make sure you know where you're going next time you book tickets otherwise you might just end up in space observing the stars from your own asteriod FUN !

      FAQ

      Quick answers about London

      Where is London located?

      London is in the United Kingdom, in Europe.

      What is the population of London?

      Greater London is home to about 9 million people, and the wider metro area to about 15.4 million.

      What is London famous for?

      London is famous for Big Ben, Buckingham Palace, the Tower of London, world-class free museums, and its West End theatres.

      Is London worth visiting?

      Yes — London was ranked #4 on Time Out's Best Cities 2026 list. Explore its facts, attractions and top things to book on this page.
