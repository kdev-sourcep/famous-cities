VALPARAÍSO











POPULATION

METRO: 1,000,000

Chile's 'Jewel of the Pacific' climbs 45 hills in a riot of painted houses, rickety funiculars and some of the best street art in South America.

HIGHLIGHT RANKING

Methodology

Before the Panama Canal, Valparaíso was the busiest port on South America's Pacific coast. Sailors from everywhere left their mark — colourful zinc-clad houses stacked on 45 hills, historic funicular elevators called ascensores, and a bohemian culture that persists.

The city is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and an open-air gallery: murals cover staircases, walls and houses across Cerro Alegre and Cerro Concepción, the two postcard hills of cafés, viewpoints and little museums. La Sebastiana, Pablo Neruda's quirky hilltop house-museum, looks over the bay.

Valpo keeps a working port's grit beneath the paint — fish markets, sailors' bars, harbour tours — and its New Year's Eve fireworks over the bay are the biggest in South America. Neighbouring Viña del Mar supplies the beaches.

FUN FACTS

45 HILLS OF COLOR

The city climbs 45 cerros, each a distinct neighbourhood of painted houses.

THE OLDEST FUNICULARS

Ascensores — hillside elevators — have carried residents uphill since 1883.

NERUDA'S HOUSE

Pablo Neruda's five-storey hideaway La Sebastiana is now a museum of his collections.

A UNESCO PORT

The historic quarter is UNESCO-listed for its intact 19th-century port cityscape.

SOUTH AMERICA'S BIGGEST FIREWORKS

New Year's Eve on the bay draws over a million spectators.

STREET ART CAPITAL

Legal murals turned whole hillsides into South America's most famous street-art destination.

A PORT THAT ONCE RULED

Before the Panama Canal opened in 1914, ships rounding the Horn made Valpo rich.

THE JEWEL OF THE PACIFIC

Chileans call it 'Valpo' — and Pablo Neruda wrote odes to it.

GRAFFITI ON EVERYTHING

Even staircases and bus shelters get painted — the city embraces it.

ASCENSOR LEGACY

Of 30 original funiculars, about a dozen still creak up the hills for a few coins.
