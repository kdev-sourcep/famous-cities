      Amsterdam

      Canals, gabled houses and bikes everywhere—Amsterdam packs Golden Age history, world-class museums and a relaxed spirit into a compact city. It is ranked #34 on Time Out's Best Cities 2026 list.

      Overview

      Why visit Amsterdam

      Amsterdam began as a small fishing village around a dam on the Amstel river in the 13th century—which is where its name comes from. During the 17th-century Dutch Golden Age it became one of the richest ports in the world, and merchants built the elegant canal houses that still line the water today. The 17th-century canal ring was added to the UNESCO World Heritage list in 2010.

      The city's museums are among the best anywhere. The Rijksmuseum holds Rembrandt's The Night Watch, the Van Gogh Museum has the largest collection of the artist's work, and the Anne Frank House preserves the secret annex where Anne wrote her diary. Book tickets ahead—they sell out quickly.

      The best way to see Amsterdam is to do as the locals do: hop on a bike or a canal boat. Explore the Jordaan's narrow streets and cafés, picnic in Vondelpark, and take the free ferry behind Central Station across the IJ to the creative NDSM area. The city has been working to manage overtourism, with limits on new hotels and campaigns encouraging visitors to respect local neighborhoods.

      Fun facts

      Things you didn't know about Amsterdam

      1. 01

        Nearly One Bike For Every Resident

        Amsterdam has roughly as many bicycles as people—by some estimates even more. Thousands of bikes are fished out of the canals every year.

      2. 02

        More Than 100 Kilometres Of Canals

        The city has 165 canals and around 1,500 bridges, more than Venice.

      3. 03

        The City Stands On Wooden Poles

        Much of Amsterdam is built on wooden piles driven into the soft ground. The Royal Palace on Dam Square alone rests on 13,659 of them.

      4. 04

        Named After A Dam

        The name comes from a dam built on the Amstel river around the 13th century—"Amstel-dam". Dam Square marks the spot today.

      5. 05

        The Canal Ring Is A UNESCO Site

        The 17th-century ring of canals around the old centre was added to the UNESCO World Heritage list in 2010.

      6. 06

        The Houses Lean On Purpose

        Many canal houses tilt forward slightly. Goods were hoisted up to the top floors with hooks on the gables, and the lean kept loads from banging into the windows.

      7. 07

        Birthplace Of The Stock Market

        In the early 1600s, shares of the Dutch East India Company were traded in Amsterdam, which is widely considered the world's first modern stock exchange.

      8. 08

        The Capital Without The Government

        Amsterdam is the capital of the Netherlands, but the parliament and government sit in The Hague.

      9. 09

        The Xxx Is Not What You Think

        The three X's on Amsterdam's coat of arms are St. Andrew's crosses. You will see them on flags, buildings and the city's bollards.

      10. 10

        The Night Watch Is Here

        Rembrandt's huge masterpiece hangs in the Rijksmuseum, where restorers have worked on it in public view so visitors can watch.
