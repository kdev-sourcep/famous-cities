WINDHOEK











POPULATION

METRO: 430,000

The safari gateway — a small German-influenced capital between deserts, the starting line for the world's most famous dunes.

HIGHLIGHT RANKING

Methodology

Windhoek is the calm before the wild — a small, orderly capital in the highlands where most travellers land before heading to the Namib Desert's giant dunes and Etosha's salt-pan wildlife.

The city itself mixes its German-colonial past (the Christuskirche's sandstone spire, the Alte Feste fort, beer-and-bratwurst eateries) with a modern African downtown around Independence Avenue. Joe's Beerhouse — the legendary game-meat beer hall — is the institution.

Speed's 2025 Africa tour streamed from Windhoek. The reason to come is what surrounds it: Sossusvlei's 300m red dunes, Etosha's wildlife and the Skeleton Coast are all reachable from here.

FUN FACTS

THE SAFARI GATEWAY

Nearly every Namibia trip routes through Windhoek.

SOSSUSVLEI

The world's tallest dunes — 300m red giants — are a day's drive west.

GERMAN ECHOES

Christuskirche, Alte Feste and bratwurst culture recall the colonial era.

JOE'S BEERHOUSE

The famous beer hall serves oryx, kudu and crocodile under fairy lights.

ETOSHA'S DOOR

The salt-pan park — Africa's great game viewing — is north of the city.

THE QUIVER TREES

Aloe 'trees' the San people used for arrow quivers dot the region.

THE SMALLEST BIG CAPITAL

Windhoek's metro is ~430,000 — small, clean, easy.

SKELETON COAST

The fog-bound shipwreck coast lies beyond Swakopmund.

DAMARA & HERERO

The Herero women's Victorian-dress tradition is a living heritage.

A CITY OF WIND

'Windhoek' means windy corner — the highland breeze is real.
