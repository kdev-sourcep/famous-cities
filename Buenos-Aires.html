      Buenos Aires

      Argentina's capital moves to the rhythm of tango — grand Parisian-style avenues, steakhouse parrillas, colourful La Boca and late-night café culture in South America's most European city. It is ranked #43 on Time Out's Best Cities 2026 list.

      Overview

      Why visit Buenos Aires

      Buenos Aires was founded twice by the Spanish, first in 1536 and again in 1580, before booming on beef and grain exports in the late 1800s. Waves of Italian and Spanish immigrants shaped the Porteño identity you hear today in the city's lilting Spanish and taste in its pizzerias.

      The city is a patchwork of barrios. La Boca's Caminito street blazes with painted tin houses and tango dancers, San Telmo fills with antique stalls on Sundays, and Recoleta's cemetery, where Eva Perón rests, is a marble city in itself. Palermo's parks and Soho streets hold the cafés, steakhouses and bars that run until dawn.

      Tango was born in the working-class ports of Buenos Aires and Montevideo, and you can still catch a milonga where locals dance until 4am. For a day out, the Tigre delta's island waterways sit an hour north by train, and a ferry crosses the Río de la Plata to Colonia del Sacramento in Uruguay.

      Fun facts

      Things you didn't know about Buenos Aires

      1. 01

        The Paris Of South America

        Buenos Aires earned its nickname from its grand boulevards, Beaux-Arts buildings and café culture.

      2. 02

        Birthplace Of Tango

        Tango emerged in the port districts of Buenos Aires in the late 1800s and is now UNESCO-listed heritage.

      3. 03

        A Cemetery That'S A City

        Recoleta Cemetery holds over 4,800 vaults, including Eva Perón's, laid out like a miniature city.

      4. 04

        The Widest Avenue

        Avenida 9 de Julio is often called the widest avenue in the world, with up to 14 lanes of traffic.

      5. 05

        More Bookshops Per Capita

        Buenos Aires has more bookshops per person than any other city, including the El Ateneo Grand Splendid theatre-turned-bookstore.

      6. 06

        It'S Mostly Italian

        Around two-thirds of Porteños have Italian ancestry, shaping the local accent, food and hand gestures.

      7. 07

        A Pink Presidential Palace

        The Casa Rosada's pink walls are said to have been mixed with cow's blood in the 1800s.

      8. 08

        The Obelisk

        The 67-metre Obelisco, built in 1936, is the city's symbol and the centre of every celebration.

      9. 09

        Football Fever

        The Boca Juniors–River Plate Superclásico is regularly called the fiercest derby in world football.

      10. 10

        Dinner At Midnight

        Porteños famously eat late — restaurants fill around 10pm and milongas get going well after midnight.
