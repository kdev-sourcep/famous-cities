      Hong Kong

      A wall of skyscrapers between green peaks and a glittering harbour, Hong Kong packs dim sum halls, double-decker trams, neon markets and island escapes into one compact city. It is ranked #15 on Time Out's Best Cities 2026 list.

      Overview

      Why visit Hong Kong

      Hong Kong was a scatter of fishing villages until it became a British colony in 1841. It returned to China in 1997 under the 'one country, two systems' framework, and that East-meets-West mix still defines it, from Cantonese temples to British-style pubs.

      The classic view is from Victoria Peak, reached by the historic Peak Tram. Down below, the Star Ferry has crossed Victoria Harbour since 1888, and the Tsim Sha Tsui promenade faces the famous skyline. On Lantau Island, the Ngong Ping 360 cable car climbs to the giant Tian Tan Buddha.

      Food is a sport here: morning dim sum, roast goose, egg tarts and late-night wonton noodles. Markets like Temple Street and the Ladies' Market glow after dark, and a third of the territory is country park, with hikes like the Dragon's Back minutes from the towers.

      Fun facts

      Things you didn't know about Hong Kong

      1. 01

        More Skyscrapers Than Anywhere

        Hong Kong has more buildings over 150 metres tall than any other city in the world.

      2. 02

        The Star Ferry

        The green-and-white Star Ferry has crossed Victoria Harbour since 1888 and still costs pocket change.

      3. 03

        Ding Ding Trams

        Hong Kong's double-decker trams have run since 1904 and form the largest double-decker tram fleet in service.

      4. 04

        A Giant Buddha

        The Tian Tan Buddha on Lantau Island is about 34 metres tall and is reached by climbing 268 steps.

      5. 05

        The Longest Escalator

        The Central–Mid-Levels escalator system is the world's longest outdoor covered escalator network.

      6. 06

        Feng Shui Towers

        Several skyscrapers have holes, called 'dragon gates', so dragons can pass from the hills to the sea.

      7. 07

        Mostly Green

        Despite the dense skyline, roughly 40 percent of Hong Kong's land is protected country park.

      8. 08

        A Handover City

        Hong Kong was British for 156 years before returning to China on 1 July 1997.

      9. 09

        Octopus Card

        Launched in 1997, the Octopus card was one of the world's first contactless payment cards.

      10. 10

        Kung Fu Capital

        Hong Kong cinema made stars of Bruce Lee and Jackie Chan and shaped action movies worldwide.
