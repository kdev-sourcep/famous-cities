AREQUIPA











POPULATION

METRO: 1,100,000

Peru's 'White City' glows with buildings of volcanic stone beneath three volcanoes, hiding the walled city-within-a-city of Santa Catalina Monastery and serving the country's spiciest food.

HIGHLIGHT RANKING

Methodology

Arequipa was founded in 1540 in a valley ringed by volcanoes — snowcapped Misti looms over the skyline. Its buildings are carved from white sillar volcanic stone, giving the 'White City' its nickname and the centre its UNESCO listing.

The city's heart is Santa Catalina Monastery, a walled 16th-century convent of painted lanes, cloisters and plazas — a city within a city that hid nuns for centuries. The Plaza de Armas, cathedral and Juanita the Ice Maiden mummy at the Santuarios Andinos museum fill the historic core.

Arequipa is also Peru's spicy kitchen: rocoto relleno, chupe de camarones and adobo come with cold chicha or a pisco sour. Beyond the city, Colca Canyon — twice as deep as the Grand Canyon — hosts Andean condors rising on the morning thermals.

FUN FACTS

THE WHITE CITY

Buildings are carved from white volcanic sillar stone that glows at sunset.

A CITY INSIDE A CITY

Santa Catalina Monastery walled off a 20,000-square-metre labyrinth of lanes, plazas and cloisters.

UNDER THREE VOLCANOES

Misti, Chachani and Pichu Pichu ring the skyline — Misti is considered active.

A CANYON DEEPER THAN THE GRAND

Colca Canyon drops over 3,000 metres — twice as deep as the Grand Canyon.

CONDORS ON THE WIND

The condors of Colca Canyon, with 3-metre wingspans, ride the morning thermals.

THE ICE MAIDEN

Juanita, a 500-year-old Inca sacrifice mummy found near-perfect on a volcano, rests in the museum.

PERU'S SPICY KITCHEN

Arequipeño food is Peru's most regional cuisine — rocoto relleno peppers are the signature dish.

AN EARTHQUAKE CITY

Arequipa has rebuilt itself repeatedly after earthquakes — 2001's badly damaged the cathedral.

A PRESIDENT'S CITY

Arequipa has produced several Peruvian presidents and calls itself Peru's 'second capital'.

SILLAR QUARRIES

The white stone is still cut by hand in the Añashuayco quarries you can visit.
