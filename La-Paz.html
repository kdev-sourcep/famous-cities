      Overview

      Why visit La Paz

      Founded in 1548 in a deep Andean canyon, La Paz is the seat of Bolivia's government and, at over 3,600 metres, the highest capital city on Earth. The valley walls rise so steeply that Mi Teleférico — the world's largest urban cable-car network — became the main public transport.

      The city is as strange as it is spectacular. The Witches' Market sells dried llama fetuses and charms for Pachamama rituals, the Moon Valley's eroded spires look lunar, and Sunday afternoons bring cholita wrestling — indigenous women in bowler hats fighting for the crowd.

      The old centre keeps colonial churches, the curve of Jaén Street's museums and the towering San Francisco basilica. Adventures radiate outward: biking the 'Death Road' down the Yungas, climbing 6,000-metre Huayna Potosí, or crossing to Copacabana and Lake Titicaca.

      Fun facts

      Things you didn't know about La Paz

      1. 01

        The Highest Capital

        La Paz's government quarter sits at 3,640 metres — visitors feel the thin air on arrival.

      2. 02

        Cable-Car Commuting

        Mi Teleférico is the world's longest urban cable-car system, with 10 lines strung across the canyon.

      3. 03

        A Market Of Magic

        The Witches' Market sells charms, herbs and dried llama fetuses buried under homes for good fortune.

      4. 04

        The Death Road

        The Yungas Road was once called the world's most dangerous — now it's a bucket-list downhill bike ride.

      5. 05

        Wrestling Cholitas

        Indigenous women in traditional skirts and bowler hats wrestle in a Sunday spectacle.

      6. 06

        A Moon On Earth

        Valle de la Luna's clay spires were said to be named by Neil Armstrong himself.

      7. 07

        A City In A Bowl

        The canyon setting means El Alto's airport, at 4,000 metres, is one of the world's highest.

      8. 08

        The Oldest Street

        Calle Jaén preserves colonial houses now turned into small museums.

      9. 09

        Indigenous Pride

        La Paz is a centre of Aymara culture — cholita fashion shows now celebrate the traditional dress.

      10. 10

        Salar Gateway

        Almost every traveller heading to the Uyuni salt flats passes through La Paz.
