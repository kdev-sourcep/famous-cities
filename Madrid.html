      Madrid

      Spain's sun-drenched capital mixes world-class art, grand boulevards and leafy parks with a legendary late-night food and social scene. It is ranked #19 on Time Out's Best Cities 2026 list.

      Overview

      Why visit Madrid

      Madrid became Spain's capital in 1561, when King Philip II moved the royal court here, and it has grown into one of Europe's liveliest cities. Grand avenues like the Gran Vía and the Paseo del Prado are lined with theatres, palaces and fountains, while the Royal Palace and the Plaza Mayor recall the city's imperial past.

      Art lovers come for the "Golden Triangle" of museums within a short walk of each other: the Prado, with masterpieces by Velázquez and Goya; the Reina Sofía, home to Picasso's Guernica; and the Thyssen-Bornemisza. Next door, El Retiro park offers rowing boats, the glass Palacio de Cristal and shady paths. Together, the Paseo del Prado and El Retiro were added to the UNESCO World Heritage list in 2021.

      Madrid truly comes alive after dark. Dinner rarely starts before 9 p.m., and neighborhoods like La Latina, Malasaña and Lavapiés are packed with tapas bars until late. On Sundays, the El Rastro flea market fills the streets, and the Madrid Río park along the Manzanares river—built over a buried motorway—is perfect for a walk or a bike ride.

      Fun facts

      Things you didn't know about Madrid

      1. 01

        Spain'S Roads Start In Puerta Del Sol

        A small plaque in Puerta del Sol marks Kilometre Zero, the point from which distances on Spain's national radial roads are measured.

      2. 02

        Home Of The World'S Oldest Restaurant

        Sobrino de Botín, founded in 1725, is recognised by Guinness World Records as the oldest restaurant in the world still operating. Its speciality is roast suckling pig.

      3. 03

        The Highest Capital In The Eu

        At around 650 metres above sea level, Madrid is the highest capital city in the European Union, which helps explain its hot summers and crisp winters.

      4. 04

        Picasso'S Guernica Lives Here

        Picasso's powerful anti-war painting hangs in the Reina Sofía museum. It only arrived in Spain in 1981, after the return of democracy, as the artist had wished.

      5. 05

        Capital Since 1561

        King Philip II moved the royal court to Madrid in 1561, turning a modest town into the capital of a global empire.

      6. 06

        El Retiro Has A Palace Made Of Glass

        The Palacio de Cristal in El Retiro park was built in 1887 to display exotic plants. Today it hosts contemporary art exhibitions.

      7. 07

        The Royal Palace Is Enormous

        With more than 3,000 rooms, Madrid's Royal Palace is one of the largest royal palaces in Europe. It is still used for state ceremonies.

      8. 08

        The Bear And The Strawberry Tree

        Madrid's symbol is a bear reaching up to a strawberry tree (El Oso y el Madroño). A statue of it stands in Puerta del Sol and is a popular meeting point.

      9. 09

        Madrileños Are Called "Cats"

        Locals born in Madrid are nicknamed gatos. Legend says it began when a soldier climbed the city walls like a cat during the conquest of 1085.

      10. 10

        A Flea Market Every Sunday

        El Rastro, one of Europe's best-known open-air markets, has been held in the La Latina area for centuries and draws huge crowds every Sunday morning.
