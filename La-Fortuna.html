LA FORTUNA











POPULATION

METRO: 20,000

The adventure capital of Costa Rica sits beneath the perfect cone of Arenal Volcano, ringed by rainforest, hanging bridges and resorts built around natural hot springs.

HIGHLIGHT RANKING

Methodology

La Fortuna began as a farming town at the foot of a mountain locals thought was dormant — until Arenal erupted without warning in 1968, burying nearby villages. The volcano rumbled and flowed for 42 years before falling quiet again in 2010, and its near-perfect cone still dominates the town.

The heat never really left. Underground volcanic activity feeds the hot springs resorts that line the road to the national park, from luxury rivers at Tabacón to simpler pools where locals soak. Above the forest, hanging bridge parks like Místico string walkways through the canopy where sloths, toucans and howler monkeys live.

The town is Costa Rica's adventure hub: whitewater rafting on the Balsa river, rappelling down canyon waterfalls, zip-lining over the jungle and hiking the old lava fields. The 70-metre La Fortuna Waterfall plunges into a jungle pool minutes from town, and vast Lake Arenal draws windsurfers and kayakers.

FUN FACTS

IT ERUPTED IN 1968

Arenal exploded after centuries asleep, wiping out two villages and staying active for 42 more years.

THE NAME MEANS LUCKY

Locals renamed the town La Fortuna — 'the fortunate one' — because the 1968 eruption flowed away from it.

NATURALLY HEATED POOLS

Arenal's underground heat warms the hot springs resorts to around 38°C without any pumps or heaters.

A 70-METRE WATERFALL

La Fortuna Waterfall drops into a jade pool reached by about 500 jungle steps.

SLOTH CAPITAL

The Bogarín Trail and nearby forests are among the easiest places on Earth to spot sloths in the wild.

COSTA RICA'S BIGGEST LAKE

Lake Arenal, enlarged by a dam, generates a large share of the country's hydroelectric power.

BRIDGES IN THE CANOPY

Hanging bridge trails rise over 40 metres, putting visitors level with monkeys and toucans.

HALF THE COUNTRY'S BIRDS

The Arenal region hosts hundreds of bird species — nearly half of all found in Costa Rica.

A YOUNG VOLCANO

Arenal is geologically young, only about 7,000 years old, which is why its cone is so symmetrical.

CHOCOLATE COUNTRY

The foothills around La Fortuna grow cacao, and farm tours show chocolate made by hand.
